How one person delivers what a team delivers

It is not talent — it is a system. When I take on a problem, there is a process that guarantees quality, speed and traceability from the first sketch to what is live.

  1. 1 · You define

    The problem in plain business terms, the definition of done and the order. The human decides what matters; no line is written before that.

  2. 2 · The system executes

    Orchestrated AI agents implement against the plan, with tests and mandatory human review before each step forward.

  3. 3 · Validation verifies

    The delivery passes automated tests, speed is measured, and anything that fails is blocked. Green is the only way forward.

  4. 4 · It goes live

    Automated publishing ships what is ready to production, with monitoring of what matters in the real environment.

Monitoring → feedback → back to step 1. Each round feeds the next: the cycle gets shorter and quality rises over time.

How I work

I work in a delivery cycle, from design to shipping — and it adapts to what you need. The first conversation costs nothing and commits you to nothing.

  1. Plan

    I define what needs to be done, the order, and the definition of done before writing a single line — from the business problem to the system boundaries.

  2. Execute

    A delivery system orchestrates the implementation with AI agents, with mandatory human review before every merge. Team quality at the pace of someone who does not wait.

  3. Validate

    Automated tests verify what matters and the cycle is measured continuously — I do not trust gut feeling, and neither should you.

  4. Deliver

    Automated publishing ships what is ready to production, with monitoring of what matters in the real environment, and feedback from live use loops back to the start — each round is faster than the last.

I do not work with a single package. The starting point is what you need to solve first, within what fits right now: we can begin with a small slice that already delivers value, grow in stages as the return shows up, and keep only a monthly support and evolution plan once it is live. A large project and a small one start from the same conversation.
